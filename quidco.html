The context

Design at scale means
every decision affects
ten million people.

Quidco wasn't a startup looking for a design direction. It was Europe's largest cashback platform, with millions of people earning real money on their everyday shopping and depending on it to work. The challenge wasn't to define something new. It was to improve something people already trusted, without breaking it.

Every change carried real blast radius. It had to work for newer users and for people who'd been on Quidco since 2008, with firm expectations about how it behaved. That demands a different discipline: more careful, more evidence-led, more respectful of what's already there.

My role was sustained, methodical improvement: working through the iOS and Android apps feature by feature, building and maintaining the shared design library, and staying close to product managers, engineers and analysts so that changes were grounded in evidence rather than opinion.

It rarely makes headlines, but it's most of what shipping and sustaining a good product actually involves, and it's work I take seriously.

Native app iteration

Ongoing improvement of iOS and Android: discovery, cashback activation, conversion funnels, offer layouts. Each change measured and validated before release.

Building and maintaining a shared component library across both platforms. Enabling faster, more consistent shipping across the whole team.

📊
Data-driven design

Close collaboration with analytics to understand what was actually happening in the product, not what we assumed was happening.

"Working on a product used by 10 million people teaches you humility. The user base is vast, diverse, and will tell you very quickly, through data, when you've got something wrong."

James Ciclitira, on Quidco

The outcome

A £101M product.
Design was part of that.

When Moneysupermarket acquired Quidco, the price reflected far more than user numbers and technology. It reflected years of accumulated trust from 10 million people. Trust like that isn't built by any single feature or screen. It's the product of thousands of small, consistent decisions holding up reliably over years.

Being part of a team that carried a product to that outcome is something I've taken into every project since.
