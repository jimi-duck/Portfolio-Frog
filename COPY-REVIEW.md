Nothing was cut for length alone. The 16% fell out of removing words that were doing

## The seven moves, in order of how often they appear

**1 · The intensifier that weakens the claim.** *significantly, effectively,
extensively, exactly, completely, even, real, key, detailed, dedicated.* These read as
though the sentence needs help. "Restructured the information architecture" is a
stronger claim than "significantly restructuring" it, because the reader supplies the
significance themselves.

**2 · The run-up.** Sentences that clear their throat before starting. "Before this
existed, ground operations ran on WhatsApp" — the section is titled *Nothing existed*.
Cut four words and the paragraph opens on the WhatsApp.

**3 · Abstract noun → the concrete thing.** *operational realities* → how the operation
actually ran. *touchpoint* → the first thing anyone saw. *a user-friendly report* → a
report advisors used on site. Every one of these was a place where the copy described a
category instead of naming what happened.

**4 · Status verb → plain verb.** *Facilitated and led* → Ran. *utilised* → used.
*provided* → carried. Same move as the earlier home-page pass (*empowers* → lets):
the plainer verb sounds more confident, not less.

**5 · The summarising tail.** Several paragraphs ended by restating what they had just
said — "The goal was simple: give users enough confidence…", "The shared design system
was what held the two together…". Cut whole. The paragraph had already landed.

**6 · Doublets.** *Redesigned and rebuilt. Secure, encrypted. Clean and consistent.
Completed and submitted. Delays and administrative overhead.* One of each pair survives.

**7 · Hedged modals.** *could drop and could break down* → dropped, failed. *Dispatchers
could create, assign and reprioritise* → created, assigned, reprioritised. The work
shipped; the past tense says so.

## Two things left alone on purpose

**The Vivy request-status section.** "Trust people with honest information rather than
paper over a messy reality with reassuring-looking UI" is the best paragraph in the four
case studies. Untouched, along with the pull quote.

**Every number, name and claim.** 70% subsidies, 25% less time on site, 80 → 95%
completeness, 5,000 scooters, one in eight cancellations, €20 minimum, the four-day
sprint, Allianz, Bosch, BEG, iSFP. The pass removed words, never evidence.

## Watch-outs, carried forward

The banned-word list from the first review still holds — *seamless, actionable, empower,
leverage, delight, elevate, passionate about* — and this pass adds four that showed up
repeatedly:

- **lightweight** — used twice in the Coup case study, both times to make a decision sound
  considerate. Say what it actually was: *one step*, *a small pilot*.
- **touchpoint** — consultancy register, same family as *operational realities*.
- **dedicated / detailed / key / real** as adjectives before a noun that doesn't need them.
  "No dedicated system" is weaker than "nothing".
- **significantly / effectively / extensively** — every one of these was cut, and no
  sentence lost meaning. If a claim needs an adverb to sound big, the claim is the problem.

One structural habit worth watching: **the summarising tail**. Four paragraphs across the
site ended by restating themselves, usually opening with *The goal was simple* or *The
shared X was what held it together*. When a paragraph has landed, stop.
