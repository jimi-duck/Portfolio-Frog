01 · Erntereif · React

A seasonal almanac for
what is actually growing near you.

When I’m food shopping, I want to know what’s genuinely in season here right now, so I can buy that instead of something flown in out of season.

87 crops across five countries, German interface. Pick a month and it shows what’s being harvested, what has come out of cold store, what’s a few weeks off, and what the imported alternative costs in carbon. Built in React and TypeScript. All 87 produce images are generated and cut from a single contact sheet, so they share one light and one camera angle.

September in Germany. 65 varieties harvested, 66 available once cold store counts

March, the same page. Nine harvested against September’s 65, and a headline that says so

Every crop carries a cooking note. Each cut-out sits on its own pale plate

02 · Tanken · SwiftUI

Fuel prices, and whether
to fill up now or wait.

When I drive past a petrol station, I want to know whether today’s price is a good one, so I don’t fill up the day before it drops.

German stations have been legally required to report their prices since 2013, so Tanken reads that public feed: today’s national average, how it got there, the five biggest chains ranked cheapest first, and a guarded outlook for tomorrow. SwiftUI, one screen, no tab bar, and the reading order is the order of the questions.

Web against native

The web loop is free.
The native one has to be built.

Erntereif needed nothing beyond a browser to check: save, reload, look.

Tanken wants Xcode, an SDK and a simulator for a build, so its logic sits in a package with no interface and can be tested without booting one.
